An absolute is a final standard — the tie post, the fixed point that everything else is measured by and nothing can move. This teaching holds that the Bible, all sixty-six books, every word of it, is that standard: God's own Word — inspired, settled, complete — and the believer's sole and final authority. Every doctrine, every creed, every experience, and every messenger — William Branham included — must be weighed against it and judged by it.

## What the Scripture Says

### God's Word, not man's

The Bible's authority rests on its authorship. It did not rise from human opinion about God; it was breathed out by God through men He moved:

<Scripture ref="2 Timothy 3:16–17">
  "All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: That the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works."
</Scripture>

<Scripture ref="2 Peter 1:20–21">
  "Knowing this first, that no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation. For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost."
</Scripture>

Because the Author "cannot lie" (Titus 1:2), the Word cannot fail: "God is not a man, that he should lie … hath he said, and shall he not do it?" (Numbers 23:19). Nor is the Word some lesser thing standing apart from God, for "in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (John 1:1) — and the psalmist says to Him, "thou hast magnified thy word above all thy name" (Psalm 138:2). To handle the Bible is to handle God's own self-expression: "thy word is truth" (John 17:17).

### Settled, unchanging, unbreakable

An absolute cannot shift, and Scripture claims exactly that permanence for itself, in both Testaments and from the Lord's own mouth:

<Scripture ref="Psalm 119:89">
  "For ever, O LORD, thy word is settled in heaven."
</Scripture>

<Scripture ref="Matthew 24:35">
  "Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away."
</Scripture>

Isaiah wrote that "the word of our God shall stand for ever" (Isaiah 40:8), and Peter, quoting him, added: "and this is the word which by the gospel is preached unto you" (1 Peter 1:25) — the enduring Word is not locked in the past; it is the very message the church carries. Jesus set its precision at the smallest stroke of the pen: "one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled" (Matthew 5:18), and settled its integrity in five words: "the scripture cannot be broken" (John 10:35). Behind that stands the unchanging God Himself — "For I am the LORD, I change not" (Malachi 3:6) — whose Word "shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please" (Isaiah 55:11).

### Complete — nothing added, nothing taken

A standard that can be amended is not an absolute. So the Word fences itself at both ends. The law opened with "Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it" (Deuteronomy 4:2), Proverbs repeats it —

<Scripture ref="Proverbs 30:5–6">
  "Every word of God is pure: he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him. Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar."
</Scripture>

— and the last book of the Bible ends under the same seal:

<Scripture ref="Revelation 22:18–19">
  "For I testify unto every man that heareth the words of the prophecy of this book, If any man shall add unto these things, God shall add unto him the plagues that are written in this book: And if any man shall take away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his part out of the book of life, and out of the holy city, and from the things which are written in this book."
</Scripture>

In its own context that warning guards "the book of this prophecy" — Revelation itself. But it is no new rule; it is the third time Scripture sets the same fence around God's words — at the giving of the law, in Proverbs, and now over the final book of prophecy God gave. The principle reaches every word He has ever spoken, wherever He spoke it: His Word is not to be edited.

Why such severity over words? Because the fall of the human race began with exactly this. The first recorded attack on man was not violence but a question aimed at the Word — "Yea, hath God said?" (Genesis 3:1) — followed by a soft amendment to it: "Ye shall not surely die" (Genesis 3:4). One word reasoned away cost Eden. The whole Bible is written against that ever happening again.

### The standard that judges everything else

When the Son of God Himself was tempted, He did not appeal to His own authority, His feelings, or His experience. Three times He answered with the same three words:

<Scripture ref="Matthew 4:4">
  "But he answered and said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God."
</Scripture>

If "it is written" was final for Jesus, nothing outranks it for us. Scripture applies that measure to every rival authority in turn. Tradition: "making the word of God of none effect through your tradition" (Mark 7:13), "teaching for doctrines the commandments of men" (Mark 7:7). Men: "let God be true, but every man a liar" (Romans 3:4). Angels: "though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel … let him be accursed" (Galatians 1:8). Spirits and spiritual experience: "believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God" (1 John 4:1). And prophets and preachers:

<Scripture ref="Isaiah 8:20">
  "To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them."
</Scripture>

The Bereans were called "more noble" for applying precisely this test to the apostle Paul — they "searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so" (Acts 17:11); "prove all things; hold fast that which is good" (1 Thessalonians 5:21). And the measure works both directions, for the Word that judges every teaching now will judge every man at the end: "the word that I have spoken, the same shall judge him in the last day" (John 12:48).

## What Brother Branham Taught

Brother Branham devoted entire sermons to this subject — among them *Absolute* (62-1230M) and *An Absolute* (63-0127) — but the conviction runs through everything he ever said. An absolute, as he defined it:

<SermonQuote title="An Absolute" date="January 27, 1963" ref="63-0127">
  "It's the last word. It's the amen."
</SermonQuote>

And his own, stated without qualification:

<SermonQuote title="An Absolute" date="January 27, 1963" ref="63-0127">
  "I've always used the Word of God, because there's no other tie-post that I know of as great as that."
</SermonQuote>

What makes that stand remarkable is whose it was. Here was a ministry marked by visions, discernment, and the supernatural from beginning to end — and he placed all of it, visions included, underneath the written Word:

<SermonQuote title="Absolute" date="December 30, 1962" ref="62-1230M">
  "If your vision's contrary to the Word, leave it alone, 'cause it's a wrong absolute."
</SermonQuote>

He also taught how the Word is understood — and it is not by councils, seminaries, or clever men. Taking Peter's words that Scripture "is of no private interpretation," he held that God needs no one to explain His Book for Him:

<SermonQuote title="Christ Is Revealed In His Own Word" date="August 22, 1965" ref="65-0822M">
  "There's no private interpreter; He does His own interpretation."
</SermonQuote>

<SermonQuote title="An Exposition of the Seven Church Ages" ref="Ch. 1 (33-3)">
  "The true revelation is God interpreting His own Word by vindicating what is promised."
</SermonQuote>

That is, God interprets His Word by bringing it to pass. When a promise written on the page happens before your eyes, no man's opinion of the verse matters any longer — God has already spoken on it.

And he applied the standard to himself, first to last. In 1953, in the years of the great healing campaigns:

<SermonQuote title="Do You Now Believe?" date="November 6, 1953" ref="53-1106">
  "And if any vision, or any person, would speak anything that was contrary to the Word, then don't hear it."
</SermonQuote>

And in 1965, months before his death, quoting Isaiah's test and putting his own name inside it:

<SermonQuote title="The Anointed Ones At The End Time" date="July 25, 1965" ref="65-0725M">
  "I don't care who he is, any other man, myself, or anybody else, if he speaks not according to the Law and the Prophets there's no Light in him."
</SermonQuote>

He preached from the King James Bible, held its sixty-six books to be the complete revelation with nothing to be added or removed, and refused every creed, catechism, and church manual as a substitute — not out of contempt for the people who hold them, but because two authorities cannot both be final.

## In Harmony with the Word

This is the one teaching on this site that needs no harmonizing — it *is* the harmony. The Bible's inspiration, permanence, completeness, and final authority are Scripture's own repeated claims about itself, from Deuteronomy to Revelation, sealed by the Lord Jesus' own use of "it is written." Every other page on this site stands on this one; it is why Scripture is set first on every page and sermon quotes stand beneath it, never beside it.

One honest question does arise: how can a people who say "the Bible alone" also receive a ministry that said "Thus saith the Lord"? The answer is in the Bible's own pattern. God has always sent messengers — "surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets" (Amos 3:7) — and He has always required them to be tested: by the written Word (Isaiah 8:20) and by whether what they say comes to pass (Deuteronomy 18:22). A true messenger never adds a new Scripture; he opens and vindicates the one already written, as John the Baptist did, whose whole ministry was a written promise made flesh. Brother Branham claimed nothing more, and invited the test at every step. This site exists to apply that test in the open, doctrine by doctrine.

## Common Questions

**If the Bible is the final authority, why follow any messenger at all?**

Because the Bible itself says God works through messengers (Amos 3:7) — while binding every one of them to the Book. The messenger's role in Scripture is never to replace the Word but to turn people back to it, the way the Bereans could search the Scriptures to confirm Paul (Acts 17:11). A message that points into the Bible honors the absolute; one that points away from it fails Isaiah 8:20 — a test Brother Branham told his own hearers to apply to him.

**Isn't the Holy Spirit our guide, rather than a book?**

The Spirit is the Guide — and He is the Author. "He shall not speak of himself" (John 16:13); the same Spirit who moved the writers (2 Peter 1:21) leads believers now, and He does not contradict Himself. Any leading, impression, or experience contrary to the Word is thereby proven not to be the Holy Spirit — "try the spirits" (1 John 4:1). Word and Spirit are not two authorities but one agreeing witness.

**Doesn't the Bible contradict itself?**

Scripture asks to be "rightly divided" (2 Timothy 2:15), and what look like contradictions are invitations to study, not flaws in the Book. Rightly placed, the passages agree — the [Water Baptism](/teachings/water-baptism/) page on this site works one famous example, Matthew 28:19 and Acts 2:38, and shows the two verses saying one thing. Brother Branham's constant counsel was that when your understanding and the Word collide, the Word is right and the understanding must yield.

**Why does this site quote the King James Version?**

For consistency with the ministry it documents. Brother Branham preached from the King James Bible, its language runs through every sermon quoted here, and it remains the common Bible of Message believers. Quoting it throughout keeps Scripture and sermon in one voice and lets every reference be checked against the same text.

That said, this is a choice of consistency, not a doctrine. Some believers hold that the King James is the only version that is truly God's Word; this site does not take that position. The absolute is God's Word itself, not one translation of it — and a faithful translation in any language is still the Word of God, as it has been for every people who ever received the Scriptures in their own tongue.
