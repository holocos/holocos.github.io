The New Testament knows one Christian water baptism: believers immersed in water in the Name of the Lord Jesus Christ. Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are not three names; they are titles of the one God, and Scripture itself identifies the Name behind all three as the Lord Jesus Christ. From the day of Pentecost onward, every baptism recorded in the Bible was performed in that Name — and this is exactly what William Branham taught and practiced.

Everything on this page can be checked. Every verse is quoted from the King James Bible, and every sermon quotation carries its tape reference so it can be read at the source.

## What the Scripture Says

### One command — and one Name

The commission itself was given by the risen Lord on a mountain in Galilee:

<Scripture ref="Matthew 28:19">
  "Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost:"
</Scripture>

Read it carefully. Jesus did not say "in the *names*" — He said "in the *name*," singular. And then notice that no name has yet been spoken in the verse. *Father* is not a name; millions of men are fathers. *Son* is not a name; it tells us whose He is, not what He is called. *Holy Ghost* is not a name; it tells us what He is — the Spirit which is holy. Three titles, one name. The command sends us looking for that Name.

### Scripture supplies the Name

The Bible does not leave the question open. Begin with the Son, whose Name was announced from heaven before His birth — with its meaning attached:

<Scripture ref="Matthew 1:21">
  "And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins."
</Scripture>

*Jesus* is the Greek form of the Hebrew *Yeshua*, a shortened form of *Joshua* — slightly different in form, identical in meaning: "Jehovah is salvation." The angel's own explanation turns on that meaning: call His name JESUS, *for* He shall save. The Father's redemptive Name is not merely alongside the Son's Name; it is inside it.

Then hear how the Son holds that Name — not as a lesser name under a greater one, but as the highest name there is, and by inheritance:

<Scripture ref="Philippians 2:9–11">
  "Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name: That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father."
</Scripture>

<Scripture ref="Hebrews 1:4">
  "Being made so much better than the angels, as he hath by inheritance obtained a more excellent name than they."
</Scripture>

God has given Jesus "a name which is above *every* name" — the verse writes in no exception. If the Father held a higher, separate name of His own, the verse could not stand as written; there is no name left above it for baptism to be performed in. And Scripture's own word for how the Son obtained His excellent name is *inheritance* — and a son inherits his name from his father. Nor does honoring that Name take anything from the Father: every tongue confessing that Jesus Christ is Lord, Philippians says, is "to the glory of God the Father."

The Holy Ghost, likewise, does not come in a name of His own:

<Scripture ref="John 14:26">
  "But the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you."
</Scripture>

The prophets had already said it would be so. Isaiah laid the titles of the Godhead on one Child: "his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace" (Isaiah 9:6). Jeremiah called the coming Son of David by the covenant Name itself: "this is his name whereby he shall be called, THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS" (Jeremiah 23:5–6). Zechariah looked to the day when "there shall be one LORD, and his name one" (Zechariah 14:9). And Peter, at Pentecost, declared His full New Testament title: "God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Christ" (Acts 2:36) — the *Lord Jesus Christ*, "for in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily" (Colossians 2:9).

### What the apostles did with the command

If any question remained about what Jesus meant, the men who heard Him say it answered that question ten days after He ascended. Jesus had told them that "repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, *beginning at Jerusalem*" (Luke 24:47), and He had given Peter "the keys of the kingdom of heaven," with the promise that "whatsoever thou shalt bind on earth shall be bound in heaven" (Matthew 16:19). At Jerusalem, on the day the church began, Peter stood up with the eleven — the same men who heard the commission — and used those keys:

<Scripture ref="Acts 2:38">
  "Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost."
</Scripture>

About three thousand souls were baptized that day (Acts 2:41). And from there, the record never varies — in every city, to every people, under every apostle's hand:

**In Samaria**, under Philip's preaching, "they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus" (Acts 8:16).

**Among the Gentiles**, at the house of Cornelius, Peter "commanded them to be baptized in the name of the Lord" (Acts 10:48).

**At Ephesus**, Paul found believers who had been baptized already — under John the Baptist's baptism — and baptized them over again:

<Scripture ref="Acts 19:3–5">
  "And he said unto them, Unto what then were ye baptized? And they said, Unto John's baptism. Then said Paul, John verily baptized with the baptism of repentance, saying unto the people, that they should believe on him which should come after him, that is, on Christ Jesus. When they heard this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus."
</Scripture>

**And Paul himself**, the apostle who received his gospel by revelation and not from man, was told at his conversion: "arise, and be baptized, and wash away thy sins, calling on the name of the Lord" (Acts 22:16).

That is the entire New Testament record. After Jesus spoke Matthew 28:19, not one person in Scripture was ever baptized any other way than in the Name of the Lord Jesus. The words "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost" are never repeated over a single convert anywhere in the Bible — not by Peter, not by Philip, not by Paul. Either the apostles unanimously disobeyed a commission some of them heard with their own ears, or they understood exactly what it meant.

<Note>
  Trace it yourself — Acts 2:38, Acts 8:16, Acts 10:48, Acts 19:5, and Acts 22:16 are every recorded Christian baptism after the commission. There is no sixth account.
</Note>

### Baptized into Him

The apostles' practice was not a preference of words. Baptism, in Scripture, is identification — being placed *into* Christ:

<Scripture ref="Romans 6:3–4">
  "Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death? Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life."
</Scripture>

"For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ" (Galatians 3:27). Paul even argued from this to the Corinthians — "was Paul crucified for you? or were ye baptized in the name of Paul?" (1 Corinthians 1:13) — baptism bears the name of the One who was crucified for you. There is "one Lord, one faith, one baptism" (Ephesians 4:5), and "none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved" (Acts 4:12). The rule covering it all:

<Scripture ref="Colossians 3:17">
  "And whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God and the Father by him."
</Scripture>

Baptism is both word and deed.

## What Brother Branham Taught

Brother Branham taught nothing on this subject beyond what the record above already shows — but he preached it plainly, at a time when almost all of Christianity baptized with the threefold titles, and he baptized every convert of his ministry in the Name of the Lord Jesus Christ. His teaching rested on the same singular "name" of Matthew 28:19:

<SermonQuote title="The Godhead Explained" date="April 25, 1961" ref="61-0425B">
  "Father is not a name; it's a title. There is no such a thing as the name of Son, because Son is a title."
</SermonQuote>

<SermonQuote title="What Shall I Do With Jesus, Called Christ?" date="November 24, 1963" ref="63-1124M">
  "Father, Son, and Holy Ghost is titles. The Name of the Father, Son, and the Holy Ghost … is the Lord Jesus Christ."
</SermonQuote>

He saw no contradiction between Matthew 28:19 and Acts 2:38 — the one is the command, the other its inspired understanding. Peter, he pointed out, was standing there when the commission was given, and held the keys to the kingdom:

<SermonQuote title="The Godhead Explained" date="April 25, 1961" ref="61-0425B">
  "Then when Peter, standing present when Matthew 28 was quoted, and turned around, and ten days later, with that revelation, and baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ … Why did he do it? With the revelation of God, and had the keys to the kingdom, brother."
</SermonQuote>

And he stated the scriptural fact bluntly:

<SermonQuote title="The Godhead Explained" date="April 25, 1961" ref="61-0425B">
  "There is not one place in the Bible where any person was ever baptized in the name of Father, Son, and Holy Ghost."
</SermonQuote>

Where, then, did the familiar practice come from? Brother Branham traced it not to the apostles but to the centuries after them, when the church organized and drifted from the apostolic pattern — through the era of the Nicene Council, as he taught at length in *The Revelation of Jesus Christ* (60-1204M) and in the Church Ages exposition. Writing of the age of Pergamos, he showed that the true church of that time was marked by holding to the Name:

<SermonQuote title="An Exposition of the Seven Church Ages" ref="Ch. 5, The Pergamean Church Age, 178-3">
  "While the many apostatized and embraced a trinity and baptized using the titles of Godhead, the Little Flock still baptized in the Name of Jesus Christ and so held to the truth."
</SermonQuote>

Two things kept his teaching balanced, and they are often missed. First, he was not "Jesus Only." He rejected the Oneness label as firmly as he rejected the idea of three persons: he taught one God in three offices — Father, Son, and Holy Ghost — and he baptized in the full Name, *the Lord Jesus Christ*, just as Peter preached Him: "both Lord and Christ" (Acts 2:36). Many men have been named Jesus; there is one Lord Jesus Christ. Second, he did not teach that water saves. The water takes away no sin — the Blood does that. Baptism in the Name is the believer's obedient answer to the Word: remission of sins preached "in his name" (Luke 24:47), exactly as the apostles carried it out.

For those who had already been baptized another way, he followed the precedent Scripture itself sets. In Acts 19 Paul did not accept even John the Baptist's baptism — a baptism ordained of God — once fuller light had come; the Ephesian believers "were baptized" again "in the name of the Lord Jesus" (Acts 19:5). On that authority Brother Branham baptized over again those who came to see the record (*What Shall I Do With Jesus, Called Christ?*, 63-1124M) — never as a judgment on anyone's sincerity, but as obedience to what the Scripture plainly shows.

## In Harmony with the Word

This teaching asks no verse to be set aside. Matthew 28:19 stands exactly as written; the question is only what obedience to it looks like — and the Bible answers its own question. There are two possible readings. Either the apostles contradicted their Lord within ten days of His command — Spirit-filled men, holding the keys of the kingdom, vindicated by three thousand souls and never once corrected in all of Scripture — or there is no contradiction at all, because "the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost" *is* the Lord Jesus Christ. Only the second reading lets every verse stand. Matthew 28:19 and Acts 2:38 are not rivals; one is the commission, the other is the commission carried out, by the men who heard it, under the inspiration of the Holy Ghost.

It should be said honestly: most of Christendom today baptizes with the threefold titles, and sincere people have done so for many centuries. This page simply lays the Scripture record beside that practice and lets the reader weigh them. Nothing here rests on Brother Branham's word alone — his teaching on baptism is, from first to last, an appeal to the book of Acts.

## Common Questions

**Doesn't Matthew 28:19 tell us to say those very words?**

It commands baptism "in the name" — singular — "of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost." The apostles who heard the command never once repeated those titles over a convert; they baptized in the Name the titles point to. If pronouncing the titles were the obedience, then Peter, Philip, and Paul all disobeyed — and heaven vindicated them anyway. The consistent witness of Acts is the Bible's own commentary on the verse.

**Is this the "Oneness" or "Jesus Only" teaching?**

No. Brother Branham stood apart from both camps: he did not teach three persons, and he did not accept the "Jesus Only" position either. He taught one God in three offices, and baptized in the Name of *the Lord Jesus Christ* — the full Name of the one God of the Bible (Acts 2:36; Colossians 2:9).

**I was baptized "in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost." Does it really matter?**

Scripture has already answered this once. The believers at Ephesus were sincere, and their first baptism had come from a man sent from God — yet when they heard the fuller light, "they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus" (Acts 19:5), and God sealed their obedience with the Holy Ghost (Acts 19:6). No one is asked to take this page's word for it; read the record, and do what they did.

**Why baptism by immersion?**

The word "baptize" comes from the Greek *baptizo* — to dip, to immerse. John baptized at Aenon "because there was much water there" (John 3:23); Philip and the eunuch "went down both into the water" (Acts 8:38); and baptism pictures a burial and rising — "buried with him in baptism, wherein also ye are risen with him" (Colossians 2:12; Romans 6:4). A burial requires covering. Brother Branham baptized believers by full immersion in the Name of the Lord Jesus Christ.
